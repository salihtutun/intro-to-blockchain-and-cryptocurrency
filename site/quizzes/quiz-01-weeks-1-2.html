Quiz 1 — Weeks 1–2 (Sessions 1–4)

Intro to Blockchain and Cryptocurrency · Fintech
Time: 15–20 minutes · Points: 20 · Closed notes · Calculator allowed
Covers: community money, keys/custody, ledgers and PoW, markets and distribution

How to take this. These are class words (cafeteria punches, house keys, a tiny chain). There are no trick questions about the news. Circle one letter. If a word is fuzzy, picture Session 1–4 — do not invent a new definition.

Name: ________________________ Team: ________ Date: ________

Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 1 pt)

Circle one letter.

A1. The Capitol Hill babysitting co-op crisis was mainly a shortage of:

  • (a) willing babysitters
  • (b) the medium of exchange (scrip)
  • (c) legal tender dollars
  • (d) a consensus algorithm

A2. In the intro version of Townsend’s unified view, a bank ledger and a blockchain are both:

  • (a) legal tender issued by a central bank
  • (b) proof-of-work systems
  • (c) databases of transfers
  • (d) community currencies by definition

A3. A digital signature on a payment primarily does which job?

  • (a) Hides the balance so others cannot read it
  • (b) Shows that the holder of the private key authorized the transfer
  • (c) Mines the next block
  • (d) Insures the sender against key loss

A4. A student sees “2 CLASSUSD” in MetaMask and “2 CLASSUSD” in an exchange app. The economic difference is:

  • (a) None — both are the student’s property in the same legal sense
  • (b) MetaMask shows keys the student controls; the exchange balance is usually a claim on the platform
  • (c) Only the exchange balance can be signed
  • (d) Only MetaMask balances are legal tender

A5. In the Naranjo-style mini-chain, prev_hash is stored in a new block so that:

  • (a) Miners can skip hashing
  • (b) Changing an old block breaks the link and fails chain verification
  • (c) The unit of account is automatically dollars
  • (d) Private keys can be recovered from the header

A6. You change one transaction in block 3 of a 10-block PoW chain and do not re-mine. verify_chain should:

  • (a) Still pass, because later blocks did not change
  • (b) Fail, because block 3’s hash no longer matches what block 4 points to (and/or PoW is invalid)
  • (c) Pass if difficulty is 1
  • (d) Recalculate difficulty automatically

A7. This course’s team coin does not need to be its own Layer-1 because:

  • (a) Tokens cannot exist without Bitcoin
  • (b) Issuance, transfer, and a shared ledger can sit on an existing testnet (Townsend: unbundle the stack)
  • (c) ERC-20 tokens cannot be transferred
  • (d) CLASSUSD already is Bitcoin

A8. A spot Bitcoin ETF and holding coins in a wallet are different mainly because they differ in:

  • (a) The mathematical definition of a hash
  • (b) Packaging: custody, fees, transferability, and who the client has a claim on
  • (c) Whether SHA-256 exists
  • (d) Whether the SEC has banned all crypto

A9. In proof-of-stake economics (Naranjo / class version), security comes mainly from:

  • (a) Electricity spent on hashing
  • (b) Capital that can be slashed if a validator misbehaves
  • (c) The number of ERC-20 decimals
  • (d) The babysitting co-op’s scrip supply

A10. Airdropping a coin, selling it, and making people earn it are:

  • (a) The same financial object with different marketing
  • (b) Different financial objects (gift / sale / wage) with different legal and incentive implications
  • (c) All automatically securities
  • (d) All automatically deposits

Part B — Short answer (2 × 5 pts)

Write in the space. Full sentences. No slogans.

B1. Community vs holder (5 pts)
A teammate says: “Our coin is an asset in my wallet, so it is not a liability.” Using the community-currency view from Session 1, explain in 4–6 sentences when that statement is incomplete. Mention one lesson from the babysitting co-op or Sarafu.

B2. Treasurer’s finality (5 pts)
A corporate treasurer asks: “After our CLASSUSD transfer shows 1 confirmation on the testnet, can I treat it as final the way a Fedwire is final?” Answer in 4–6 sentences. Use probabilistic finality and at least one operational caveat (reorg, wrong network, or pause).
