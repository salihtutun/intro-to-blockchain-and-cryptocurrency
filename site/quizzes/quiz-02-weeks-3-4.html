Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 1 pt)

Circle one letter.

A1. In the intro version of Townsend’s circulating-private-debt story, uncoordinated private IOUs are a problem because:

  • (a) IOUs cannot be written without Ethereum
  • (b) Agents cannot easily know what to issue or how others will accept it, so the system can fail to coordinate
  • (c) All private money is illegal
  • (d) Smart contracts forbid IOUs

A2. A smart contract, as used in this course, is best described as:

  • (a) A lawyer’s PDF
  • (b) Pre-agreed code that can hold and move value when stated conditions are met
  • (c) A proof-of-work puzzle
  • (d) A central-bank reserve account

A3. On Ethereum, a transaction that reverts (fails) typically:

  • (a) Costs nothing
  • (b) Still consumes gas / fees, even though state did not update as intended
  • (c) Prints new ETH for the sender
  • (d) Finalizes in Bitcoin

A4. The point of the ERC-20 interface for this class is that:

  • (a) Every coin must have its own blockchain
  • (b) Wallets, CLASSUSD, and the class AMM can treat every team coin the same way (transfer, balance, approve)
  • (c) Tokens cannot have a cap
  • (d) Solidity must be written from scratch

A5. The pauser role on a team coin is closest to which fintech job?

  • (a) Market-making intern
  • (b) Compliance / operations freeze when something is wrong
  • (c) Custodian bank’s vault guard only
  • (d) ETF authorized participant

A6. CLASSUSD in this course is the unit of account. A team coin is primarily a:

  • (a) Central-bank reserve
  • (b) Competing rail / privately issued money that is priced in CLASSUSD
  • (c) Proof-of-work algorithm
  • (d) Legal tender in the United States

A7. “Priced in dollars, settled in the coin” means:

  • (a) The invoice number is in USD and the payment rail can be the token
  • (b) The token is legal tender
  • (c) Gas is free
  • (d) No AMM is allowed

A8. A constant-product pool holds x TEAM and y CLASSUSD with k = x \cdot y fixed (no fees). If someone buys TEAM, then:

  • (a) x rises and y rises
  • (b) x falls and y rises, so the CLASSUSD-per-TEAM price rises
  • (c) k must change by law
  • (d) Slippage is always zero

A9. Impermanent loss, in the Naranjo / Uniswap intuition, is:

  • (a) The gas fee
  • (b) The gap between holding the two assets vs providing them as liquidity when prices move
  • (c) A slash on a validator
  • (d) The Howey test

A10. Seeding a TEAM/CLASSUSD pool and then swapping is “sharing in the system” because:

  • (a) The instructor deletes other coins
  • (b) Classmates can get a quoted price and trade without a designated human market maker
  • (c) ERC-20 forbids transfers
  • (d) The coin leaves the testnet automatically

Part B — Short answer and calculation (10 pts)

B1. Coordination (4 pts)
In 4–6 sentences: how does a shared minting rule (cap, minter role, roster airdrop) solve a problem that “each student prints their own IOU on paper” does not? Use the class system, not a slogan.

B2. AMM trade (6 pts)
A fee-less constant-product pool has 100 TEAM and 100 CLASSUSD, so k = 10{,}000.

A classmate buys TEAM by adding CLASSUSD until 90 TEAM remain in the pool.

  1. (2 pts) What is the new CLASSUSD reserve?
  2. (2 pts) How much CLASSUSD did the classmate pay (ignore gas)?
  3. (2 pts) The spot price after the trade is CLASSUSD per TEAM. What is it, and why is it not 1.00?
