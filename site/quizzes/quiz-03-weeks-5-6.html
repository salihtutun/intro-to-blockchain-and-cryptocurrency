Part A — Multiple choice (10 × 1 pt)

Circle one letter.

A1. “The token is a receipt for an off-chain asset” means:

  • (a) The token is the T-bill
  • (b) Someone must custody the real asset and honor redemption; the token is a claim
  • (c) No legal wrapper is needed
  • (d) Atomic settlement is impossible

A2. Atomic delivery-versus-payment (DvP) is attractive versus a brokerage trade fail because:

  • (a) Gas is always zero
  • (b) Asset and payment legs can succeed together or not at all
  • (c) Howey no longer applies
  • (d) Oracles cannot exist

A3. A drawback Townsend flags (intro version) when only the current holder can program an asset is:

  • (a) Hashes stop working
  • (b) Entering a trade can reveal information or create a hold-up
  • (c) CLASSUSD cannot exist
  • (d) Banks cannot use Excel

A4. On the money flower, CLASSUSD in this class is closest to:

  • (a) Physical cash issued by a sovereign
  • (b) A privately issued digital token used as the class unit of account (not a real CBDC)
  • (c) Gold in a vault at Fort Knox
  • (d) A share of SPY

A5. In a three-team obligation triangle (A owes B 10, B owes C 10, C owes A 10), multilateral set-off can:

  • (a) Create 30 of new CLASSUSD
  • (b) Cancel the cycle and leave zeros if amounts match
  • (c) Require proof-of-work
  • (d) Convert the debts into Bitcoin

A6. A stablecoin depeg in market data (Naranjo) is evidence of:

  • (a) Perfect arbitrage at all times
  • (b) Price leaving the intended peg; frictions, reserve doubt, or a run may be at work
  • (c) ERC-20 decimals changing
  • (d) A successful Howey defense

A7. In the class AMM, Team P is a “key player” (intro Townsend/networks) if:

  • (a) They have the funniest ticker
  • (b) Others need their pool for price and liquidity; a pause or drain spills over
  • (c) They never airdropped
  • (d) They used proof-of-work for the ERC-20

A8. Under a simple Howey-style lens, a campus coin is more likely to look like an investment contract if promoters:

  • (a) Use it only as a closed voucher for a good the team actually delivers
  • (b) Sell it to outsiders with the pitch that a team of managers will work to raise its price
  • (c) Keep it on testnet with no sale
  • (d) Call it a “utility token” in a tweet

A9. The FATF Travel Rule, in one sentence for this course, is about:

  • (a) Airline miles
  • (b) Originator/beneficiary information traveling with certain crypto transfers, like wire information
  • (c) Mining difficulty
  • (d) Uniswap fees

A10. This course’s hard rule is:

  • (a) Deploy on Ethereum mainnet in Week 3
  • (b) Testnet only; the compliance memo asks “what if we left the lab,” the deploy never does
  • (c) Sell tokens to the public for USD
  • (d) Skip KYC discussion because it is not fintech

Part B — Short answer (2 × 5 pts)

B1. Classify this coin (5 pts)
A team sells “EAGE” (Eagecoin) to students for CLASSUSD, promises 1 EAGE = one coffee the café will honor off-chain, and also tweets “Eagecoin will 10× when we list on a real exchange.” In 5–7 sentences, classify Eagecoin (voucher / e-money-like / security-like / mixed) and name one feature you would cut before any real launch.

B2. Stress the system (5 pts)
The instructor announces that CLASSUSD minting is paused and one large TEAM/CLASSUSD pool is drained to a thin book. In 5–7 sentences, describe (i) what happens to quoted prices, (ii) who gets hurt (LPs vs holders vs payers), and (iii) one kill criterion a risk officer would write before this week.
